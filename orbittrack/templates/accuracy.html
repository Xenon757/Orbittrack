03 How to read this

The reference. NASA's Johnson Space Center trajectory office publishes the ISS's position and velocity every 4 minutes for about 15 days (CCSDS Orbit Ephemeris Message, EME2000 frame). It's built from tracking and high-fidelity force models.

What's a measurement. At the start of NASA's file the position comes from recent tracking, so the difference there is a real measure of the TLE's error.

What's a forecast. Later points are NASA's prediction, so the growing distance is two models disagreeing, mostly about future atmospheric drag. It includes NASA's own forecast uncertainty.

Why along-track. The ISS ends up in the right place on its orbit, just early or late, because drag is what a TLE models least well. That's why pass times are shown to the second but drift if the TLE is old.
